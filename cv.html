Summary

I am a PhD Scholar in Natural and Applied Sciences working at the intersection of environmental systems, human activity and technology. My doctoral research focuses on large-scale prediction and risk assessment of groundwater contamination by integrating hydrogeochemistry and environmental geoinformatics with remote sensing, GIS and multi-source environmental data. I work across uranium, fluoride and arsenic contamination, using machine learning, deep learning, AI and spatial modelling to investigate contamination patterns, environmental controls and spatially differentiated risk. My broader interests include population exposure, environmental vulnerability, groundwater governance, climate and cryosphere systems, environmental change and water security. I am particularly interested in interdisciplinary research that connects spatial science and emerging technologies with real-world environmental and societal challenges, and remain open to new questions, methods and collaborations.
