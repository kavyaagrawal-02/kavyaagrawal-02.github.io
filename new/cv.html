About

I am a PhD scholar in Natural and Applied Sciences at the TERI School of Advanced Studies, New Delhi, working at the intersection of environmental systems, human activity and technology. My doctoral research focuses on large-scale prediction and risk assessment of groundwater contamination, integrating hydrogeochemistry and environmental geoinformatics with remote sensing, GIS and multi-source environmental data.

I work across uranium, fluoride and arsenic contamination, using machine learning, deep learning, AI and spatial modelling to investigate contamination patterns, environmental controls and spatially differentiated risk. My broader interests include population exposure, environmental vulnerability, groundwater governance, climate and cryosphere systems, environmental change and water security.

Research areas

Current

  • Uranium

    National-scale prediction of uranium in groundwater across India

    AutoML (AutoGluon) prediction with independent validation, spatial risk classes, population exposure and groundwater governance indicators for decision support.

  • Fluoride

    India-wide fluoride prediction and the effect of spatial resolution

    Benchmarking deep learning models; predictor stacks built at native, 500 m, 1 km and 5 km scales from Google Earth Engine.

  • Arsenic

    Arsenic mobilisation in the Middle Gangetic Plain

    Distribution, speciation and geogenic controls; pH, iron, redox potential and sulfate; explainable AI (SHAP, LIME).

  • Downscaling

    Physically informed downscaling and upscaling of satellite predictors

    Sub-pixel fractions and conservation-based aggregation, piloted on CHIRPS precipitation over Punjab (2013–2022); validated with Moran's I, semivariograms and aggregation consistency.
