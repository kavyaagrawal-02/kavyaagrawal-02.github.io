About

I work where Earth observation, water and people meet. As a PhD scholar at TERI School of Advanced Studies, I build national-scale machine-learning frameworks that predict groundwater contamination across India, from uranium and fluoride to arsenic, and turn those predictions into maps of risk, population exposure and governance priorities.

My toolkit brings together remote sensing, GIS, Google Earth Engine, hydrogeochemistry, machine learning and deep learning. Along the way I have studied land surface temperature, permafrost and snow in the Indus Basin, worked on downscaling satellite data, taught remote sensing and GIS to MSc students, and served as a rapporteur at the World Sustainable Development Summit.

I want to carry these methods to bigger questions: climate change and the cryosphere in High Mountain Asia and the polar regions, water security, and even reading history and archaeology from space. I am always glad to hear about new ideas and collaborations.

Research streams

National-scale uranium assessment

A national-scale model of where uranium occurs in India's groundwater, checked against an independent validation dataset and turned into maps of risk, population exposure and governance priority.

Paper submitted to journal; Best Paper Award, EHP NEXUS 2026

Fluoride and spatial resolution

India-wide fluoride prediction that benchmarks deep learning models and tests how the resolution of environmental predictors (native, 500 m, 1 km, 5 km) changes what a model can see.

In progress

Arsenic in the Middle Gangetic Plain

Distribution, speciation and controls on arsenic mobilisation, with explainable AI used to check that model explanations stay physically plausible.

Presented at As2024, Bhubaneswar

Downscaling satellite predictors

A physically informed framework for moving environmental data between coarse and fine resolutions without inventing detail, piloted on CHIRPS rainfall over Punjab.

In progress
