Research

Predicting groundwater contamination across India, and what it means for the people who drink it.

The doctoral framework

My PhD, Development of an Adaptable Machine Learning Based Framework for Multi-Contaminant Prediction, asks how far we can predict groundwater contamination across a country as large and varied as India, and what those predictions mean for the people who drink the water.

The framework joins groundwater observations and hydrochemistry with remote sensing and GIS layers on climate, hydrology, terrain, soil, geology, land use, human modification and population. Machine learning, deep learning and automated machine learning do the prediction; spatial statistics, independent validation and explainable AI check whether the predictions hold up and make physical sense.

Three contaminants

Uuranium

Predicting where uranium occurs in groundwater throughout India with AutoML, then mapping risk, population exposure and groundwater governance priority for decision support.

Submitted to journal Best Paper Award 2026
Ffluoride

Benchmarking deep learning models for India-wide fluoride prediction, and testing how spatial resolution (native, 500 m, 1 km, 5 km) changes what a model can see.

In progress
Asarsenic

Distribution, speciation and controls on arsenic mobilisation in aquifers of the Middle Gangetic Plain, including the roles of pH, iron, redox potential and sulfate.

Presented at As2024

Scales I work across

Contamination models draw on Earth observation data recorded at very different resolutions. Part of my work is aggregating them honestly to a common grid without quietly losing groundwater observations. These are the native resolutions of some of the datasets in the fluoride predictor stack.

Downscaling environmental predictors

A related paper in progress builds a physically informed framework for multi-resolution downscaling and upscaling of environmental predictors from satellite data. A pilot on CHIRPS precipitation over Punjab (2013–2022) uses sub-pixel fractions and conservation-based aggregation to separate genuine spatial downscaling from simple resampling, checked against Moran's I, semivariograms and aggregation consistency.

Earlier work: mountains and ice

Before groundwater, I worked on the cryosphere and climate of the Himalaya and Indus Basin: daytime and nighttime land surface temperature trends by elevation (published in the Journal of Mountain Science, 2023), identifying and mapping permafrost in the Indus Basin from roughly twenty years of MAAT, PISR, BMAT, snow and precipitation estimates in Google Earth Engine, and snow cover change in Indian basins.
